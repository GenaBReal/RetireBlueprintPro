What if the market crashes the year you retire?

We put Michael and Linda's plan through a 20% crash, a 30% crash, high inflation and 5,000 random market futures. The steady crash tests left them short of their legacy goal but not out of money, and staying flexible made the biggest difference.

By Gena Philpott, founder of RetireBlueprint Pro · Updated October 2026

The short answer: A crash in the first year of retirement shrinks what Michael and Linda leave behind, from about $100,000 to $74,000 after a 20% drop and $60,000 after a 30% drop, but their money still lasts their whole lives. Across 5,000 random market futures, if they spend every planned extra no matter what, their money lasts in 41% of them. If they trim their extras by 10% in down years, their essential bills stay covered in 89%.

Why the first years matter most

Financial planners call this sequence of returns risk. If the market drops right when you start withdrawing, you sell investments while prices are low, and that money isn't there when the market recovers. The same crash ten years later does much less damage, because by then you've already taken out a good chunk of what you need.

The planner replays their whole retirement with a single bad year at the start, then normal returns after that. Here's what's left at the end of the plan:

Test 2: 5,000 random futures

Real markets don't have just one bad year. A Monte Carlo simulation runs the plan through thousands of possible futures, with good years, bad years and crashes in random order, and counts how often the money holds up.

How they spendResult across 5,000 futures
Spend every planned extra dollar, no matter whatMoney lasts in 41%
Trim the extras by 10% in down yearsEssentials covered in 89%

The difference is largely about flexibility. Their essential bills don't change. The only thing that changes is whether they hold back a little on travel and fun money after a bad year.

What "trimming the extras" means for them: 10% of their $22,497 early-years extra is about $2,250 a year, under $190 a month, and only in years after the market falls.

What helps if a crash hits early

  • Flexible "extras": splitting spending into must-pay bills and nice-to-haves gives you something to adjust that doesn't touch your bills.
  • A cash cushion: Michael and Linda keep $35,000 in emergency savings, so they don't have to sell investments at the bottom.
  • Guaranteed income: Social Security and Linda's pension pay out whatever the market does, so the worse the market, the more these matter.
  • Testing before, not after: knowing your numbers ahead of time turns a scary headline into a plan you already made.

Quick answers

What is sequence of returns risk?

It's the risk that the market falls early in your retirement, when withdrawals do the most damage, because you sell investments while prices are low.

How much does a 30% crash in the first year hurt a retirement plan?

For our sample couple, a 30% drop in year one cut what they leave at the end from about $100,000 to about $60,000, but their money still lasted their whole lives.

What is a Monte Carlo retirement simulation?

It runs your plan through thousands of possible market futures in random order and reports how often the money holds up. Michael and Linda's essentials stayed covered in 89% of 5,000 futures when they trimmed their extras by 10% in down years.
