What will healthcare cost in retirement?

Healthcare is the bill most people underestimate, especially before Medicare starts at 65. Here are the real numbers in Michael and Linda's plan, and where to check your own.

Before 65: the coverage gap

If you retire before Medicare, you need your own coverage. The three usual options are:

  1. A working spouse's employer plan, often the cheapest if it's available.
  2. COBRA: you keep your old plan for up to 18 months, but you pay the full premium yourself.
  3. The Marketplace at HealthCare.gov, where premium help depends on your income. Withdrawals and Roth conversions count as income, so your retirement plan and your health insurance cost are tied together.

Linda retires first, in 2027, and Michael keeps working into 2028. Until Medicare starts at 65, their plan budgets about $1,300 a month for healthcare.

Two things often catch people off guard:

  • Routine dental, vision and hearing aren't covered by Original Medicare. Many retirees buy separate plans or set aside a dental fund.
  • IRMAA: if your income is high, including from large withdrawals or Roth conversions, your Part B and D premiums go up. If your income drops after retiring, you can ask Social Security to lower it with Form SSA-44.

Where to check your own numbers

Quick answers

How much does Medicare cost per month for a couple?

For our sample couple, Part B, Part D and a Medigap supplement come to about $686 a month for both, or $8,230 a year. Check Medicare.gov for this year's official premiums.

What does health insurance cost if I retire before 65?

It depends on your options: a spouse's employer plan, COBRA, or a Marketplace plan where premium help depends on income. Our sample couple budgets about $1,300 a month in their early 60s.

Does Medicare cover dental and vision?

Original Medicare does not cover routine dental, vision or hearing. Many people buy separate coverage or choose a Medicare Advantage plan that includes it.
